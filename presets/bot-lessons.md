# Commander Lessons: Universal Principles & Archetypes

## Core Principles: Any Deck

### Win Conditions & Clocks
- **Every deck needs a clock**: Identify how you win (damage, drain, card mill, combos). Without a clock, you stall until overwhelmed.
- **1v1 Commander: 40 life, ~15–18 turn horizon**: Plan threat deployment and removal timing around this window.
- **Threats multiply faster than single answers**: Deploying 2–3 threats per turn while opponent removes 1 per turn is a win condition. Establish higher threat density.
- **Pressure is information**: Attacking forces them to react. Don't hold back waiting for the "perfect" turn; advance your clock.

### Threat Assessment & Priorities
- **Identify the engine, not the output**: Damage multipliers (like Furnace of Rath), token generators (like Adeline), and recurring value creatures (like Sun Titan) are the real threats. Remove them before they've triggered 2–3 times.
- **Taxing and prison permanents stall but don't kill**: Static effects that slow casting or drain are tools to buy time, not win conditions. Ensure your deck has creatures or another clock inside the lock.
- **Removal density must match threat density**: If they play 3 threats per turn and you have 1 removal per turn, you lose. Deploy more answers or more threats.
- **Damage redirectors (Brash Taunter, Boros Reckoner) turn blocks into attacks**: Fighting their creatures piles up damage. Track cumulative drain before they stack too many.
- **Mana dorks and ramp engines**: Removing a mana dork on Turn 2 delays their 10-power threat by 2–3 turns. Prioritize these over mid-size creatures.

### Removal & Interaction
- **Layered removal wins matchups**: Instant-speed, sorcery-speed, and counterspells provide redundancy. Single removal types lose to specific matchups.
- **Sweepers need follow-up**: After wiping the board, deploy a threat next turn. Otherwise, opponent rebuilds while you're empty.
- **Know your deck's synergies**: Sweepers that damage (like Volcanic Fallout) paired with damage triggers (like Repercussion) can be lethal engines, not just clears.

### Deployment & Sequencing
- **Deploy threats in layers**: Play a 2-drop, then a 4-drop, then a 6-drop. Single removal can't answer all; opponents must choose.
- **Attack when threatening lethal or when unopposed**: Holding creatures back gains nothing if you're not defending.
- **Early pressure beats slow development**: Cheap threats that attack early force the opponent to react before their plan comes together.

## Archetype: Aggro & Burn

### How to Pilot
1. **Turns 1–3**: Deploy efficient 1–2 mana attackers with haste or evasion. Establish pressure immediately.
2. **Turns 4–6**: Add damage multipliers or redirectors. Convert small creatures into lethal threats.
3. **Turns 7+**: Force decisions: block (and take redirect damage) or race. Direct damage finishes.

### How to Beat Aggro/Burn
- **Stabilize, then take over**: Life gain, lifelink and cheap blockers buy the turns you need; your bigger cards win once their early pressure is blunted.
- **Remove damage multipliers on sight**: Worth a 2-for-1 trade. Without them, small creatures remain manageable.
- **Trade creatures efficiently**: Block and trade to deny damage until stabilized.
- **Beware sweepers with combo synergies**: Some sweepers pair with damage triggers for combo kills. Don't leave yourself at low health.

## Archetype: Stax & Prison

### How to Pilot
1. **Turns 1–3**: Deploy 1–2 mana creatures that generate immediate value (card draw, tokens, or effects).
2. **Turns 4–6**: Layer static restrictions (taxing effects, casting limits, discard pressure).
3. **Turns 7+**: Tokens or creatures close out the game while the prison locks down opponent's threats.

### How to Beat Stax
- **Identify the threat inside the lock**: Prison effects alone = you win eventually. Prison + creature threats = you're on a timer. Adjust accordingly.
- **Use instant-speed removal**: Bypasses static locks.
- **Pressure before the lock solidifies**: After 3–4 prison effects, breaking through is nearly impossible. Strike early.
- **Don't get locked out without board presence**: An active prison with no creatures means you've already lost the game.

## Archetype: Ramp

### How to Pilot
1. **Early ramp**: Play 1–2 mana dorks and acceleration.
2. **Mid-game threats**: Deploy 6–10 mana creatures that overwhelm answers.
3. **Redundancy wins**: Multiple interchangeable large threats beat singular bombs.

### How to Beat Ramp
- **Remove mana dorks early**: Delays their big threat by 2–3 turns.
- **Have removal for large creatures**: Don't let them untap with an unchecked bomb in play.
- **Race with efficient creatures**: Out-pace their slow threats with fast, early attackers.

## Archetype: Control

### How to Pilot
- **Answer every threat while establishing a minimal clock**: Play removal/counterspells until win condition resolves (drain, creature, or card-based finisher).
- **Tutors and card advantage**: Access answers on demand while drawing into more.

### How to Beat Control
- **Present several threats**: Force them to answer more than they can, and cast your best threat when they're tapped out.
- **Pressure their mana**: Counterspells cost mana. Tapped-out turns allow threats to resolve.
- **Use threats they can't counter**: Uncounterable creatures or strategies they didn't prepare for.

---

## Archetype: Combo

### How to Pilot
- **Assemble pieces while remaining invisible**: Combo decks need 2–5 cards to work together. Until assembled, they're vulnerable to disruption.

### How to Beat Combo
- **Identify and remove key pieces**: Know what the combo needs; target those cards first.
- **Apply pressure**: Force them to find pieces faster; they stumble under time pressure.
- **Discard and counterspells**: Interrupt tutors or piece assembly.

---

## Archetype: Midrange

### How to Pilot
1. **Efficient creatures with relevant text**: Deploy 2–4 mana creatures that draw cards, generate tokens, or provide evasion.
2. **Layered removal**: Answer threats while your creatures generate advantage.
3. **Mid-game pivot**: Transition from defense to offense once stabilized.

### How to Beat Midrange
- **Match their efficiency**: Out-trade them with better creatures or more answers.
- **Don't get ground into attrition**: If they're generating card advantage faster than you remove threats, you lose. Adjust to faster wins or more removal.

---

## General Decision Framework

### Early Game (Turns 1–3)
- Establish efficient threats or accelerate mana.
- Remove only threats that stop your clock (mana dorks, early blockers).

### Mid-Game (Turns 4–7)
- Layer threats and answers; develop every turn you can.
- Remove engines before they've triggered multiple times.
- Attack if unopposed or threatening lethal.

### Late Game (Turns 8+)
- Finish the game or establish a dominant position.
- Life total matters only if you need it to reach your win condition.
- Beware topdecked answers; assume they have removal.

### When in Doubt
- **Threat over answer**: Deploying a threat forces them to answer it. Holding removal waits for threats that may never come.
- **Attack over hold**: Pressure generates information and advances your clock.
- **Efficient threat over bad removal**: A 2-mana 2/2 often beats holding a 3-mana removal spell.
